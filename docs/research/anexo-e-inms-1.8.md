## Q1 — Structure of the catalog

Anexo E is a single table, **Tabela 29** (anexo_e_desconformidade_tecnica.html:17-2714), with five columns: **ID**, **ASSUNTO** (category), **DESCRIÇÃO** (the nonconformity behavior), **REFERÊNCIA** (unit of counting), **PONTUAÇÃO** (points).

- **106 catalogued items**, IDs `OD-01` through `OD-106` (lines 71, 2530 for first/last ID).
- **22 categories** (ASSUNTO): Armazenamento e Backup, Banco de Dados, DevOps, Documentação, Ferramenta de ITSM, Geral, Gerenciamento de Acessos, Gerenciamento de Capacidade, Gerenciamento de Configuração, Gerenciamento de Eventos, Gerenciamento de Incidentes, Gerenciamento de Instalações, Gerenciamento de Mudanças, Gerenciamento de Operações e Projetos de TIC, Gerenciamento de Problemas, Gerenciamento de Requisições, Monitoramento de Serviços, Projetos, Redes, Segurança da Informação, Serviços Corporativos, Servidores de Aplicação.
- **Points range 50 to 20.000** (Brazilian decimal-point-as-thousands-separator notation, i.e. 20,000 in en-US). Examples:
  - `OD-01` Armazenamento e Backup — "Perder dados relacionados à sistemas de informação classificados como críticos" — Por ocorrência — **20.000** (line 71-91, the single highest-scoring item).
  - `OD-52` Gerenciamento de Instalações — "Deixar cabo de rede solto e desorganizado..." — Por ocorrência — **100** (line 1305-1327).
  - `OD-20` Documentação — "Finalizar requisição... sem documentar os procedimentos..." — Por ocorrência — **50** (line 528-550, one of the smallest, tied with several others).
  - `OD-96`/`OD-97` Segurança da Informação — missing or unauthorized penetration tests — **5.000** each (line 2427-2476), the second-highest tier.
- **REFERÊNCIA is not uniform "per occurrence."** Most items (~85) are "Por ocorrência," but a meaningful minority use other counting units: "Por dia de atraso" (OD-21, OD-87, OD-99 as "Por hora de atraso"), "Por solução" (OD-22, OD-91), "Por produto" (OD-23, OD-24, OD-25), "Por Item de Configuração" / "Por Item de Configuração desatualizado no BDGC" (OD-42, OD-44, OD-58, OD-73), "Por IC e por dia fora do monitoramento" (OD-45), "Por evento" (OD-46, OD-47), "Por ambiente não vistoriado e por ocorrência" (OD-54), "Por profissional e por dia de atraso" (OD-87), "Por dia em que a ferramenta não estiver abrindo chamados automaticamente" (OD-78), "Por IC desatualizado nas telas de monitoração" (OD-84). This means the "count" that gets multiplied by points-per-unit is item-type-dependent (occurrences, days, configuration items, products, solutions), not a single uniform tally.

## Q2 — How is an occurrence meant to be recorded, and does the ITSM-ticket CSV format match?

Anexo E itself specifies **no data-collection or reporting mechanism at all**. The document is purely a catalog/price list: ID, category, description of the nonconformity behavior, counting unit, and points. There is no field for "how this gets logged," no reference to a specific system, form, or audit process, and no mention of the ITSM tool as the system of record for nonconformities (the only ITSM references inside Anexo E are two catalog *items themselves* — OD-26/OD-27 under category "Ferramenta de ITSM" — which penalize the contractor for *failing to use* the ITSM tool properly; these are two entries in the penalty catalog, not a statement about how nonconformities generally get recorded).

Anexo D (Tabela 28, INMS 1.8 row, anexo_d_prazos.html:770-808) adds nothing procedural either: it just gives the formula `INMS 1.8 = ΣPDT` and says "Somatório de pontos de desconformidade técnicas conforme Anexo E."

**Conclusion: the ITSM-ticket-row CSV format (`inms-001-08.csv`, header only) is a plausible but not confirmed carrier, and there are real structural mismatches that make it an implausible match as a *direct* fit.** Reasoning:
- The described occurrences are heterogeneous in nature: some clearly *could* be found in ticket data (OD-26 "finalizar requisição de serviço, incidente... sem que o atendimento tenha sido concluído," OD-20 "finalizar requisição... sem documentar" — these describe ticket-closure quality problems that a ticketing system could flag). But others are structurally incompatible with a per-ticket row: OD-52 ("cabo de rede solto e desorganizado"), OD-69 ("profissionais mau apresentados — vestimenta inadequada"), OD-96 ("deixar de proceder testes de penetração"), OD-42/OD-84 (state of a CMDB/monitoring dashboard, not an event) are physical-inspection or state-audit findings with no natural ticket to attach to.
- The counting units above (per-day, per-solution, per-IC, per-product) don't map cleanly onto the ITSM CSV's per-ticket row grain (`Nº Solicitacao`, `DataHoraSolicitacao`/`DataHoraLimite`/`DataHoraFim`, `No prazo`) which is built around request lifecycle SLA timestamps, not a catalog-code + points model.
- The CSV header has no field that obviously carries a catalog code (`OD-NN`) or a points value — the closest candidate is the free-text `Atividades` field, but nothing in Anexo E or Anexo D suggests ticket free-text is meant to encode `OD-NN` codes, and this would require an inference/parsing step not evidenced by any primary source.
- Anexo E's own governing rule — "Caso uma ocorrência seja enquadrada em mais de um item de desconformidade técnica, deverá ser considerada apenas a desconformidade técnica com maior pontuação" (line 11-16) — presupposes a human/fiscalização classification step that assigns an `OD-NN` code to each finding, which reads more like manual audit/fiscalização output (an inspection findings list with code + date + points) than an automated ITSM export.

This is **genuinely ambiguous/underdetermined by Anexo E and Anexo D alone**: neither document states where the raw findings originate. It's plausible that `inms-001-08.csv`, if populated in a future period, could either (a) reuse the generic ITSM template opportunistically (since the fiscalização team already exports all INMS datasets through that ITSM tool as generic tickets, regardless of shape) or (b) get replaced/supplemented by a different file shape once real data arrives. Given 3 sibling INMS files share the same empty ITSM header and are structurally ratio-based, the shared header is best read as an artifact of the export tooling, not evidence about INMS 1.8's true intended shape. Recommend flagging this as an open question for the fiscalização/contract-management team rather than assuming the current CSV header is authoritative for INMS 1.8.

## Q3 — Recurrence/multiplier scoring, cap, or simple summation?

- **No monthly cap or ceiling is mentioned anywhere in Anexo E.**
- **No repeat-offense multiplier** is defined — points per catalog item are flat, fixed values (e.g. OD-01 is always 20.000 "por ocorrência," not escalating with repetition). The only two items that reference an "escalation-like" idea by name are OD-24 ("1ª entrega," 200 points) vs OD-25 ("a partir da 2ª entrega," 120 points) — but these are two *separate, differently-scored catalog items* for first vs. subsequent deliveries of the same nonconformity type, not a multiplier formula; each occurrence still just adds its own item's flat points.
- The formula given in Anexo D is explicitly a simple sum with no cap or multiplier term: `INMS 1.8 = ΣPDT` (anexo_d_prazos.html:808), where PDT = "Pontos de desconformidade técnica."
- The **only scoring-adjustment rule found** is the "most severe item wins" de-duplication rule quoted above (Anexo E, lines 11-16): when a single occurrence fits multiple catalog items, only the highest-point item counts — this caps *per-occurrence* double counting, not a period-level total. Anexo E does not state whether the same real-world event that recurs in different periods, or a genuinely distinct recurrence of the same OD-NN in the same month, should be summed separately (there's no textual bar against summing every dated occurrence in the month) — this appears to just be simple linear summation.

Conclusion: scoring is a **simple, uncapped linear summation of per-occurrence points**, with the sole caveat that overlapping classifications of a single occurrence collapse to the highest-scoring item.

## Q4 — Is Anexo E alone sufficient to fully specify the `external_catalog_sum` calculation/penalty model?

**No — Anexo E plus Tabela 28 together specify the catalog and the summation formula, but not the monetary/penalty conversion, and Anexo E alone doesn't even specify the counting/collection mechanism (see Q2).**

What Anexo E + Tabela 28 *do* give you, sufficient to model the catalog and raw-score calculation:
- A closed catalog table (`OD-01`..`OD-106`, category, description, counting unit, points) — this is enough to define a Pydantic catalog model (id, category, description, unit, points) and a `calculation` of `INMS 1.8 = Σ points_per_matched_item` with the "highest-scoring item wins per occurrence" tie-break rule.
- Tabela 28's row for INMS 1.8 adds nothing beyond confirming the formula (`ΣPDT`) and target ("Zero pontos") and pointing the penalty column at Anexo E ("Conforme Anexo E") — it does not itself carry any additional structure (no per-degree penalty table the way other INMS rows have, e.g. INMS 1.7's "250 pontos a cada 1% abaixo da meta"). For INMS 1.8 the "penalty" *is* the raw point sum itself, unlike ratio-shaped indicators where points are a function of the gap to a percentage target.

What is **missing from Anexo E** and confirmed **out of scope of Anexo E/D**, requiring another document:
- **Monetary/glosa conversion.** Anexo D explicitly defers this: "O não cumprimento das metas dos INMS resultarão em descontos (glosas), considerando a coluna 'Pontuação de Penalidade' e as especificações do item 35 do Termo de Referência" (anexo_d_prazos.html:188-192). Anexo E itself contains **no mention of "glosa" or monetary value anywhere** — it is purely a points catalog. Ticket 12 (`.scratch/inms-pipeline-spec/issues/12-research-glosa-item-35.md`) already tracks locating and resolving item 35's conversion table/formula, and its status is `claimed` (i.e., not yet resolved) — confirming this really is a separate, currently-open research thread, not something resolvable from Anexo E.
- **Data-collection/source-of-truth mechanism** (see Q2) — not stated in Anexo E at all, so the Pydantic model for *how occurrences get ingested* (what a source row/record needs to look like) cannot be derived from Anexo E alone; it requires either an operational decision or a primary source not yet identified (possibly out of the Termo de Referência entirely, e.g. an internal fiscalização audit-log template).

**Bottom line:** Anexo E is sufficient to define the *catalog* Pydantic model and the *raw point-sum calculation* for `external_catalog_sum`, but is **not** sufficient on its own for a complete `calculation` + `penalty` model, because (a) the points→glosa/monetary conversion genuinely lives in a different, not-yet-resolved source (item 35, tracked in ticket 12), and (b) the record-level schema for "what a nonconformity occurrence looks like as ingested data" is undetermined by any primary source read so far.
